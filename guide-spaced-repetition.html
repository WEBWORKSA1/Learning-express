Spaced Repetition Explained: The Schedule That Beats Cramming

How the forgetting curve works, a simple spaced-repetition schedule you can follow on paper, and how to combine it with flashcards.

In the 1880s, psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus memorised lists of nonsense syllables and measured how quickly he forgot them. The result, now called the forgetting curve, shows memory dropping steeply in the first days after learning and then levelling off. Each time we review at the right moment, the curve flattens further. Spaced repetition is simply the practice of reviewing just before you would forget.

Cramming vs. spacing

Cramming can work for tomorrow’s test, but most of it disappears within a week. Spreading the same total study time over several sessions — the spacing effect — is one of the most consistently replicated findings in learning research. You do not need to study more; you need to study at better times.

A simple schedule you can use today

ReviewWhenTypical time
LearnDay 0Full session
Review 1Day 110 minutes
Review 2Day 38 minutes
Review 3Day 76 minutes
Review 4Day 145 minutes
Review 5Day 305 minutes

If a review goes badly, restart that item’s schedule from Day 1. If it goes easily, you can stretch the next gap. Our spaced-review scheduler calculates these dates for any start date.

Using flashcards well

  • One idea per card. Small cards are easier to recall and to diagnose.
  • Write in your own words. Copying textbook sentences invites recognition rather than recall.
  • Add context. A short example or image cue makes cards more memorable.
  • Review daily, briefly. Ten minutes every day outperforms an hour once a week.
Express tip: Do your review first thing in your study session while attention is fresh, then move to new material.

What to space

Spacing is ideal for facts, vocabulary, formulas and definitions, but it also helps with skills. Revisit a coding exercise a week later, re-solve a maths problem type after a few days, or re-explain a concept without notes. The principle is the same: retrieve after a delay.
