Career Switch Roadmap: Change Fields in Six Months

A month-by-month plan for moving into a new field: choose a target role, close skill gaps, build proof and land interviews.

Changing careers feels risky because it is usually done all at once. A staged plan reduces the risk: you test the new field, build evidence while still employed and apply only when you can show results. Here is a six-month framework.

Month 1: Choose a target role

Shortlist three roles using official data on duties, pay and outlook (O*NET, the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook or Job Bank Canada). Talk to two people currently doing each job. Choose the one where your existing skills transfer best.

Month 2: Map the skills gap

Collect 15 job ads and tally the skills and tools they mention. Mark which you already have. The rest become your curriculum — most can be learned on an Express Line.

Months 2–4: Learn by building

Study 6–10 hours a week and produce something at every stage: an analysis, a website, a campaign, a case study. Each project should mirror a task from those job ads.

Month 4: Package your story

  • Rewrite your CV around transferable achievements with numbers.
  • Publish a simple portfolio page with two or three projects.
  • Write a one-sentence pivot story: “I moved from retail operations to data analysis because…”.

Month 5: Get warm introductions

Referrals are often the fastest route to interviews. Send short, specific messages to people in the field, offer something useful and ask for advice rather than a job. Volunteer or freelance small projects to gain references.

Month 6: Interview and negotiate

Prepare ten STAR stories (situation, task, action, result) and practise aloud. Research salary ranges so you can negotiate with data. Ride the Career Launch Line for practice resources.

When formal training helps: regulated fields and some technical roles need a credential. Use Program Match to compare certificate, bootcamp and degree routes for your target role.
